AI Workforce UX Case Study

Designing AI-Assisted Shift Change Management for Retail Stores

How I redesigned shift changes from request processing into explainable, AI-assisted workforce decisions.

Role Senior Product Designer
Timeline April 2026
Project Type Take-home / Workforce SaaS
Tools Figma, UX strategy, AI product thinking

Problem Context

Shift changes sit at the intersection of coverage, cost, compliance, and fairness

In retail workforce management, a shift change is not just a request. It affects store coverage, labor cost, payroll accuracy, regional rules, and the trust associates place in the scheduling system.

Users

Store managers

Need to make fast decisions while balancing floor coverage, payroll risk, and compliance.

Users

Hourly associates

Need transparency, fair outcomes, and clear status when they request a shift change.

Business

HR and payroll

Need clean traceability so corrections do not become manual cleanup at period close.

The product opportunity was to make the right decision obvious, defensible, and fast: show the decision context, recommend the best path, and record the rationale automatically.

My Role & Approach

I treated the assignment as a product strategy problem, not only a UI redesign

01

Mapped the workflow

Request, review, approve, execute, and reconcile were mapped across associate, manager, and payroll touchpoints.

02

Prioritized product risks

I focused on trust collapse, compliance blind spots, and manager cognitive overload as the highest-risk failures.

03

Defined automation boundaries

Routine, reversible requests could be automated. Uncertain or high-liability decisions needed human review or escalation.

04

Connected AI to outcomes

AI was framed around measurable workflow improvements: faster decisions, fewer payroll errors, and smarter coverage recovery.

UX Analysis

The same system problem appeared differently for each stakeholder

01

Request

Associate submits a swap but has limited visibility into status or likely outcome.

02

Review

Manager sees a queue but lacks inline coverage, cost, and compliance context.

03

Approve

Decision happens without a clear rationale, making escalations hard to defend later.

04

Execute

Schedule updates need to propagate cleanly without creating new payroll exceptions.

05

Reconcile

HR and payroll often absorb the cost of missing records through manual cleanup.

Information scarcity

Managers make approval decisions without the payroll impact, coverage change, and compliance status in one place.

Opaque outcomes

Associates get denied without useful explanation, which pushes people toward text messages and offline workarounds.

Weak traceability

Actions happen, but the system does not always preserve a readable record of why the decision was made.

Design Direction

A decision-first workflow with risk, context, and accountability built in

Decision reversibility
Human review Same-day cover
Auto-approve Same-role swap
Elevated approval Payroll lock, union override
Manager confirm Bid fill, time off in budget
Automation confidence

Risk-ranked manager inbox

Requests are sorted by urgency and risk instead of arrival time.

Inline decision signals

Coverage, payroll delta, compliance, and availability appear before the approval action.

Explainable audit trail

Every human and system action writes a plain-language record that can be reviewed later.

Alternative paths

Denied requests become ranked alternatives, not dead ends.

AI-Led Product Improvement

AI becomes the system's decision-support layer, not a decorative feature

The strongest product opportunity was to use AI to reduce manual search, detect hidden risk earlier, and help managers act before coverage problems become urgent. The AI recommends, explains, records, and escalates when confidence is low.

Predictive AI

Ranked-candidate auto-suggester

Instead of forcing managers to hunt through schedules, the system ranks the top replacement options using role fit, availability, overtime headroom, seniority, and coverage impact.

Anomaly Detection

Time-punch anomaly flagging

Repeated late starts, early clock-outs, or rounding patterns are flagged before payroll locks.

Agentic AI

Predictive coverage-gap alerts

An agent watches the next 72 hours against forecasted demand and proposes fill requests before gaps appear.

Explainability

Recommendations with rationale

Each automated action explains why it happened: same role, no overtime, coverage above target.

Final Solution

The final experience helps managers decide faster without hiding accountability

Step 1

See what needs attention

The manager opens a risk-scored queue where urgent, high-liability requests rise above routine swaps.

Step 2

Understand the decision

The review card brings coverage impact, payroll delta, compliance status, and AI recommendation into one view.

Step 3

Approve a defensible action

The manager selects the best path, the schedule updates, and the system writes the rationale automatically.

Reflection

This assignment demonstrates product judgment beyond screen design

The case study shows how I think through enterprise UX: understand the operating constraints, define the decision model, design for edge cases, and use AI where it can improve outcomes instead of simply adding novelty.

The core lesson is that AI product design has to stay accountable. The system can automate and recommend, but the experience must still explain, escalate, and preserve trust across every stakeholder.
