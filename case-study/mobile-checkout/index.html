Ecommerce UX Case Study

Designing a High-Converting Mobile Checkout Experience

How I reduced mobile cart abandonment by simplifying checkout, building trust, and removing friction.

Ecommerce Conversion Optimization Mobile UX

Problem

High mobile traffic but weak checkout conversion was driving revenue leakage

~70%+

Mobile traffic share

~2.9%

Mobile conversion rate

~70%

Cart abandonment at checkout

The business was losing revenue because mobile checkout abandonment (~70%) and low mobile conversion (~2.9%) were significantly underperforming expected benchmarks.

Goal & Scope

Tight scope on checkout flow to improve conversion-critical behavior

Primary Goal

  • Reduce mobile cart abandonment.

Secondary Goals

  • Increase checkout completion rate.
  • Reduce payment error recovery drop-offs.
  • Improve accessibility compliance to WCAG AA.

Scope

  • Focused only on checkout flow optimization.
  • Product page -> Cart -> One-page mobile checkout.
  • Traffic acquisition and discovery were out of scope.

Research & Insights

Competitive pattern audit + secondary research from Baymard

Competitive Insights

Reference review: Amazon, Shopify, Stripe

No forced account creation

Move account creation after payment confirmation.

Progressive disclosure

Information -> Shipping -> Payment to reduce perceived length.

Trust near CTA

Lock icon and secure microcopy directly under Pay Now action.

Secondary Research (Baymard)

Top checkout abandonment reasons

19% Lack of trust in entering card details
19% Forced account creation
18% Checkout too long or complex

Design Strategy

Three friction drivers mapped to concrete UX decisions

Block 1 - Trust

Users hesitated while entering payment details

Problem

Trust anxiety reduced willingness to complete payment.

Solution

  • Add visible trust signals close to payment action.
  • Add "Secure Checkout" microcopy near CTA.
  • Use padlock icon directly under payment action.

Block 2 - Account Creation

Forced sign-up created avoidable drop-offs

Problem

Users resisted registration during checkout.

Solution

  • Enable guest checkout as default path.
  • Move account creation after purchase completion.
  • Position sign-up with clear value benefits.

Block 3 - Complex Checkout

Long forms increased friction and error risk

Problem

Too many visible fields increased cognitive load and form errors.

Solution

  • Use progressive disclosure with accordion sections.
  • Remove nonessential fields.
  • Add real-time validation and browser autofill.
  • Support third-party wallets for faster completion.

Key Takeaways

What this case reinforced

  • Checkout issues are UX problems, not traffic problems.
  • Trust is emotional and visual, not only technical.
  • Guest checkout improves conversion over forced registration.
  • Progressive disclosure reduces form friction without reducing required data.
  • Microcopy at hesitation points directly impacts revenue behavior.

Conclusion

Conversion improves when you remove friction, not when you add features.

When most users arrive on mobile, each forced login, unclear trust cue, and extra field silently taxes revenue. In this case study, meaningful improvement came from making checkout feel safer, shorter, and more respectful of user time.
